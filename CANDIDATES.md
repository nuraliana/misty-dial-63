# Candidates — Funniest Time Machine Warning Label

Scored against the five judge criteria, 5 points each, 25 total:

1. A genuine warning label for a time machine.
2. A real hazard of time travel turned into the joke, not named flatly.
3. The surprise saved for the last few words.
4. A straight face, phrased like a genuine safety notice.
5. No wasted words inside the character limit.

No candidate reaches a genuine 25/25. The best score is 24/25, shared by
four lines; the winner is picked on the tie-break below. Scores were not
inflated to manufacture a perfect score.

## The candidates

### 1. WINNER — 24/25 (5, 5, 5, 5, 4) — 80 characters

> WARNING: Your return ticket departed yesterday. Enjoy your new life in the past.

Rationale: the stranded-forever hazard is embodied, never named; the punch
("new life in the past") lands in the last four words with the highest stakes
of any candidate. Criterion 5 loses one point: "Enjoy your new life" carries
two beats for one punch.

### 2. — 24/25 (5, 4, 5, 5, 5) — 72 characters

> WARNING: In case of emergency, the nearest exit is 400 years behind you.

Rationale: perfectly tight and deadpan, but the joke is a location gag about
the exit's address rather than the emergency hazard itself, so criterion 2
loses a point.

### 3. — 20/25 (4, 4, 4, 5, 3) — 95 characters

> WARNING: Warranty expired 200 years before this machine was built. File claims before purchase.

Rationale: two sentences repeat the same warranty joke, diluting the punch,
and "this machine" never says time machine outright.

### 4. — 24/25 (4, 5, 5, 5, 5) — 80 characters

> WARNING: Do not feed the dinosaurs. They have already eaten the last tour group.

Rationale: dark and tight, but without time-travel context it could be a zoo
or safari label, so criterion 1 — the most important criterion — loses a
point.

### 5. — 19/25 (4, 3, 3, 5, 4) — 77 characters

> WARNING: Your luggage will arrive before you do. Please do not unpack it yet.

Rationale: early luggage is a mild hazard and "do not unpack it yet" is a
weak, confusing punch rather than a surprise.

### 6. — 21/25 (4, 4, 4, 5, 4) — 83 characters

> WARNING: Calls made from this machine are answered 40 years before they are placed.

Rationale: a decent time-paradox gag, but "this machine" is vague, the
surprise is mid-weight, and "made from this machine" is wordy.

### 7. — 20/25 (4, 3, 4, 5, 4) — 98 characters

> WARNING: This machine is older than its own invention. Spare parts are no longer made, or not yet.

Rationale: "or not yet" is a nice twist, but spare parts are a weak hazard
and "this machine" never says time machine outright.

### 8. — 24/25 (5, 4, 5, 5, 5) — 86 characters

> WARNING: In case of paradox, please follow the instructions you have not yet received.

Rationale: strong and tight, but it names the hazard ("paradox") flatly in
the opening clause instead of embodying it, so criterion 2 loses a point.

## Tie-break

Candidates 1, 2, 4 and 8 tie at 24/25. Criterion 1 is the most important, so
candidate 4 drops first (its label could be a zoo sign). Of the rest,
candidate 1 wins on criterion 2, which outranks criterion 5: it is the only
tied line that turns the hazard (stranded one-way, forever) into the joke
without naming it, and it ends on the biggest-stakes punch. Candidate 1 is
the entry.
