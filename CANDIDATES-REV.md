# Revision candidates — Funniest Time Machine Warning Label

Scored honestly against the 5 judge criteria (5 points each, 25 total):
1. A warning label for a time machine. 2. A real time-travel hazard turned into the joke, not named flatly.
3. Surprise saved for the last few words. 4. Straight face, like a genuine safety notice.
5. No wasted words — every phrase doing work.

## Variant 1 — WINNER — 25/25 (5/5/5/5/5) — 86 chars

> WARNING: Your return ticket departed yesterday. Welcome to the rest of your life here.

Rationale: the stranded hazard arrives via the departed ticket, never named flatly; the forever-reveal lands in the final words; "Welcome" keeps the official calm while "rest of your life here" carries permanence and place with no slack word (no "new", no "in the past").

## Variant 2 — 23/25 (5/4/5/5/4) — 94 chars

> WARNING: Return travel is unavailable from your destination. Your new address is this century.

Rationale: strong closing image, but "unavailable" names the hazard flatly and "from your destination" / "new" carry slack.

## Variant 3 — 22/25 (5/5/4/4/4) — 82 chars

> WARNING: Your return ticket expired before you left. Enjoy the past — permanently.

Rationale: the expired-before-you-left paradox is a good joke, but "Enjoy" breaks the official calm slightly, telegraphs the punch, and does little work beyond tone.

## Variant 4 — 23/25 (5/5/5/4/4) — 85 chars

> WARNING: The return journey leaves before you arrive. Pack for the rest of your life.

Rationale: good temporal joke and ending, but "Pack" reads as travel advice rather than a safety notice, and the first sentence is wordier than the ticket version.

## Variant 5 — 22/25 (5/4/4/5/4) — 97 chars

> WARNING: No return service operates from this era. Please make permanent arrangements on arrival.

Rationale: "permanent arrangements" is a nice euphemism, but the opening names the hazard flatly and "Please" / "on arrival" are slack at the end.

## Variant 6 — 23/25 (5/4/5/5/4) — 80 chars

> WARNING: Return tickets are collected on arrival. This century is now your home.

Rationale: shortest and bureaucratic, but the plural generic tickets lose the personal stake and "this century" leaves past vs. future ambiguous.

## Decision

Only Variant 1 reaches a genuine 25/25. It is the line written by `main.py`.
